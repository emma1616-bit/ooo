Personal Portfolio Website — Product Requirements Document

1. Product Overview

Product Name

Personal Portfolio Website

Primary Use Case

Undergraduate application portfolio for programs related to:

Bioengineering

Neuroscience

Philosophy as a secondary intellectual dimension

Primary Audience

University admissions officers.

The website should not function as a conventional resume archive or a collection of unrelated achievements. Its purpose is to help an admissions officer understand the applicant as a complete person: intellectually curious, scientifically rigorous, creative, empathetic, disciplined, and aesthetically sensitive.

Core Product Goal

Create a memorable, editorial-style personal website that combines research, social impact, art, performance, and everyday practices into one coherent personal narrative.

The website should leave the reader with the impression:

This is someone who uses science, art, and lived experience as different ways of understanding people.

2. Positioning

2.1 Core Positioning

The portfolio should position the applicant primarily as:

A future bioengineering / neuroscience student with strong interdisciplinary curiosity and a distinctly human-centered perspective.

Philosophy should not appear as an isolated academic identity.

Instead, philosophical thinking should emerge naturally from scientific questions involving:

consciousness

mental health

technology

privacy

identity

human experience

The website should therefore avoid presenting three disconnected academic interests.

Instead, it should communicate one broader intellectual direction:

Understanding people through science, technology, observation, and expression.

3. Core Narrative

Narrative Theme

Understanding People

The website is built around three connected ideas:

Science helps me investigate people.

Art helps me observe and express people.

Empathy helps me understand why any of this matters.

The applicant's diversity of interests should not be presented as “I can do many things.”

The deeper message should be:

My interests are different expressions of the same curiosity.

Research, drawing, calligraphy, dance, podcasting, advocacy, running, cooking, and baking are connected through recurring behaviors:

observation

experimentation

interpretation

discipline

expression

care

4. Desired Personality Impression

The website should communicate six core personal qualities.

Curiosity

Demonstrated through scientific research, interdisciplinary questions, and exploration beyond formal coursework.

Rationality

Demonstrated through research methods, data analysis, experimental processes, academic writing, and structured thinking.

Creativity

Demonstrated through visual art, content creation, interdisciplinary thinking, design, dance, and alternative ways of presenting research.

Empathy

Demonstrated especially through eating-disorder advocacy, podcasting, mental-health-related work, and attention to human consequences of technology.

Discipline

Demonstrated through long-term art practice, calligraphy, dance training, fitness, research, and sustained personal projects.

Aesthetic Sensibility

Demonstrated through art, visual presentation, photography, handwritten notes, composition, and the visual identity of the site itself.

These words do not need to be explicitly listed on the homepage.

The content should prove them.

5. Experience Principles

The website should feel like:

an interdisciplinary personal magazine rather than an academic database.

The experience should balance:

intellectual depth

personal warmth

visual curiosity

readability

restraint

Recommended balance:

70% structured editorial layout

30% expressive / unexpected composition

Creative elements should never reduce readability for admissions officers.

6. Content Hierarchy

The hierarchy should prioritize:

Tier 1 — Research and Intellectual Identity

Highest importance.

Includes:

EEG research

RIMI Lab internship

Online-user depression prediction

BCI-related philosophical reflection

Tier 2 — Social Impact and Empathy

High importance.

Includes:

eating disorder awareness project

treatment-oriented educational content

personal podcast

original illustrations

Tier 3 — Artistic Identity

Important for differentiation.

Includes:

painting

sketching

calligraphy

dance

Tier 4 — Everyday Practices

Supporting personality layer.

Includes:

baking

cooking

running

fitness

These should provide personality rather than compete with the academic work.

7. Recommended Site Architecture

7.1 Home

Purpose:

Introduce the applicant emotionally before introducing achievements.

The homepage should immediately communicate:

science + art + personality

rather than presenting a conventional profile photo and biography.

Opening Hero

Preferred format:

Dynamic collage

The hero should combine selected fragments such as:

EEG waveform

handwritten research notes

painting detail

calligraphy

dance still

podcast waveform

running data

food / baking image

personal photography

The collage should feel curated rather than chaotic.

It should visually demonstrate that multiple identities belong to one person.

Opening Copy

The opening statement should be short.

Recommended tone:

reflective, intelligent, personal, understated.

Example direction:

I study signals, people, and the spaces between them.

Supporting line:

Through neuroscience, bioengineering, art, movement, and storytelling, I keep returning to the same question: how do we understand one another?

Exact copy can be refined later.

8. Homepage Structure

Section 01 — Introduction

Purpose:

Answer:

Who is this person?

Content may include:

short personal introduction

current intellectual interests

visual collage

handwritten annotation

optional subtle portrait

Avoid a long biography.

Section 02 — Questions I Keep Asking

Purpose:

Introduce the intellectual worldview before individual projects.

Possible questions:

How can a signal become an understanding of a person?

What can technology know about our mental states?

Where does scientific measurement stop and human experience begin?

Can observation itself become a form of empathy?

This section should bridge bioengineering, neuroscience, and philosophy.

Section 03 — Research

Purpose:

Establish academic credibility.

Featured projects:

Project 01

EEG Data Collection

Core theme:

Measuring the invisible

Focus on:

research objective

EEG methodology

experimental setup

signal collection

workflow

personal contribution

problems encountered

iteration

research notes

lessons learned

Visual assets:

EEG plots

experiment setup

research notes

diagrams

workflow

paper excerpts

Project 02

RIMI Lab Internship

Core theme:

Learning how research works

Focus less on institutional prestige and more on:

what the lab investigates

applicant responsibilities

research environment

methods learned

collaboration

new questions generated

personal growth

This project should reveal:

How I behave inside a real research environment.

Project 03

Online User Depression Prediction

Core theme:

When data becomes personal

Content structure:

Research problem

Motivation

Data / methodology

Prediction model

Findings

Limitations

Ethical questions

Personal reflection

This project should intentionally transition from technical research into philosophy.

Possible transition:

If online behavior can reveal a person's mental state, the scientific question is no longer the only question.

Then explore:

privacy

inference

consent

identity

mental health

responsibility

This is the strongest place to introduce BCI and philosophical thinking.

9. Research Project Page Template

Each important research project should have a dedicated detail page.

Recommended structure:

01. Question

What did I want to understand?

02. Context

Why does this problem matter?

03. Process

What did I actually do?

04. Evidence

Data, diagrams, screenshots, experimental material.

05. My Role

Clearly distinguish personal contribution from team activity.

06. Obstacles

What failed or became difficult?

07. Iteration

What changed?

08. Outcome

What did the project produce?

09. Reflection

What did I learn?

10. New Question

What question did the project leave me with?

The final section is especially important.

It creates continuity between projects.

10. Social Impact Section

Working Title

Empathy in Practice

Alternative titles:

Caring About People

Beyond Observation

When Understanding Becomes Action

This section should feature the eating-disorder awareness initiative prominently.

Key Content

why the project was created

anorexia awareness

educational content

treatment information

platform development

podcast

illustrations

personal responsibility as founder

one-year development timeline

audience / engagement metrics if meaningful

The project should not be presented as charity for the purpose of application.

The emphasis should be:

I encountered a human problem that mattered to me, and I chose to build something around it.

11. Podcast Presentation

Podcast content should feel intimate and human.

Possible UI concept:

A visual audio waveform appears alongside:

episode title

one-sentence theme

selected quote

illustration

Interaction could allow users to move across the waveform or explore different episodes.

The site should not autoplay audio.

12. Art Section

Working Title

Ways I See

The art section should not resemble a competition certificate gallery.

It should show the evolution of observation.

Painting and Drawing

Content may move chronologically:

Childhood → technical observation → form → light → color → composition.

Show:

oil painting

sketches

studies

selected process work

Avoid displaying too many pieces.

Curate approximately:

6–10 strong works.

13. Calligraphy

Calligraphy should be positioned around:

control

patience

rhythm

structure

negative space

Possible connection to scientific work:

Both research and calligraphy require precision, but also interpretation.

Include:

hard-pen calligraphy

brush calligraphy

selected competition work

close-up details

process / practice sheets if visually strong

14. Dance

Possible Theme

Thinking Through Movement

Dance experience includes:

Chinese dance

ballet

street dance

Rather than simply listing styles, show the contrast between them.

Possible storytelling structure:

Chinese dance → expression and control
Ballet → structure and discipline
Street dance → freedom and improvisation

Assets:

performance photos

competition images

rehearsal photos

short video clips if available

Motion can be used more strongly here than elsewhere.

15. Everyday Life Section

Working Title

Things I Keep Doing

This section should be lighter and more personal.

It may include:

Running / Fitness

Show selected longitudinal data.

Possible visuals:

yearly distance

frequency

streaks

pace progression

simple route lines

The goal is not athletic performance.

The goal is to communicate consistency.

Baking / Cooking

Present food as a form of experimentation and care.

Possible mini-story:

recipe → adjustment → process → final result.

Avoid turning the section into a food blog.

Select only visually strong examples.

16. Final Personal Section

Working Title

A Few More Things About Me

This section gives the admissions officer a quieter ending.

Possible content:

small habits

favorite forms of making

books or questions currently thinking about

sketchbook fragments

handwritten notes

recent curiosities

It should feel like discovering the person behind the application.

17. Final CTA

The closing section should remain minimal.

Include:

short closing sentence

CV

email / contact

optional LinkedIn if appropriate

Do not use aggressive CTA language such as:

“Hire Me”

or

“Work With Me.”

Recommended tone:

Thank you for spending some time inside the way I see the world.

19. Visual Direction

Overall Style

Editorial magazine + personal research notebook

The site should feel:

bright

refined

warm

experimental

intelligent

personal

Avoid:

corporate blue

generic university style

dark “tech portfolio” aesthetics

excessive glassmorphism

excessive futuristic effects

overly childish scrapbook styling

20. Color System

Base

Warm off-white / cream.

Example direction:

ivory

paper white

light warm beige

Avoid pure white dominating the entire interface.

Accent Colors

Use a small number of bright colors.

Potential direction:

vivid red / coral

cobalt / electric blue

fresh green

warm yellow

Do not use all of them equally.

Recommended structure:

1 primary bright accent + 1 secondary accent + occasional project-specific colors.

Color should appear in:

annotations

highlight blocks

interactive states

project categories

diagrams

21. Typography

Use a strong editorial hierarchy.

Recommended combination:

Primary Typeface

Clean sans serif.

Used for:

navigation

body copy

research information

captions

Secondary Typeface

Editorial serif.

Used sparingly for:

large statements

reflections

philosophical questions

quotes

Personal Layer

Actual handwritten material.

Rather than using an artificial handwriting font, scanned or photographed handwriting should be used whenever possible.

This makes the visual identity genuinely personal.

22. Layout

The layout should follow:

Controlled asymmetry.

Use:

large margins

overlapping images

unexpected image placement

changing text widths

handwritten arrows

floating annotations

occasional rotated elements

But body text and academic content should remain stable and easy to scan.

Rule:

Exploration for images, discipline for information.

23. Motion Design Principles

Motion should communicate meaning.

Avoid animation purely for decoration.

Recommended Interactions

Hero Collage

Different fragments subtly respond to mouse movement.

EEG

Waveform animates gently during scroll or hover.

Research Notes

Annotations appear progressively as the visitor reads.

Project Connections

Handwritten lines or arrows connect related ideas.

Art Gallery

Images behave like pages from a portfolio book.

Dance

Sequential still frames can create movement.

Podcast

Waveform responds to interaction.

Running

Route or line gradually draws while scrolling.

Navigation

Transitions should feel smooth and editorial rather than app-like.

24. Interaction Intensity

Three levels:

Level 1 — Essential

Reading and navigation.

Must remain extremely clear.

Level 2 — Discovery

Hover interactions, annotations, small reveals.

Encourages curiosity.

Level 3 — Delight

Occasional unexpected moments.

Examples:

a sketch shifting slightly,
a note being “written,”
a waveform responding,
a photo appearing behind a sentence.

Level 3 should remain rare.

25. Mobile Requirements

Admissions officers may open the website from laptops, tablets, or phones.

The design must therefore remain fully usable on mobile.

On mobile:

collage simplifies

overlapping elements reduce

text stays linear

animation intensity decreases

research content becomes vertically structured

Nothing academically important should depend on hover.

26. Content Tone

All content should be written in English.

The tone should be:

intelligent

concise

reflective

curious

human

non-performative

Avoid language that sounds like:

“I am passionate about…”

“I have always dreamed…”

“I am a highly motivated student…”

Instead, use specific observation.

Example:

Weak:

I have always been passionate about neuroscience.

Better:

The first EEG signal I collected looked less like an answer than another question.

27. Writing Style for Project Pages

Recommended pattern:

Start with a question.

Create intellectual curiosity.

Explain the context.

Keep it short.

Show the work.

Use visuals generously.

Explain personal contribution.

Be concrete.

Reflect.

This is where personality appears.

End with another question.

Create continuity.

28. Content Length Guidelines

Admissions readers are time-constrained.

Recommended:

Homepage introduction:
40–80 words

Homepage project descriptions:
30–60 words

Research case study:
400–800 words

Social project:
300–600 words

Art section:
minimal text, image-led.

Everyday life:
very short captions.

30. Asset Selection Principle

Do not upload everything.

For each category, select material based on:

Does this tell the reader something new about me?

If two images communicate the same thing, keep the stronger one.

The portfolio should feel curated.

31. Homepage Success Criteria

Within approximately the first minute, an admissions officer should understand:

The applicant is strongly interested in neuroscience / bioengineering.

The applicant has real research experience.

The applicant thinks beyond technical questions.

The applicant cares about mental health and people.

The applicant has sustained artistic interests.

The applicant is highly multidisciplinary.

These interests feel connected rather than random.

32. Product Success Criteria

The website succeeds if it makes the applicant easier to remember after reviewing many candidates.

The desired memory is not:

“The student who does many activities.”

It is:

“The student who connects science, people, and expression.”

33. Key Design Risk

Risk 1 — Too many interests

The website can easily become fragmented.

Solution:

Every section must connect to the central idea of understanding people.

Risk 2 — Art overwhelms academics

Because visual work is naturally attractive, it may receive disproportionate attention.

Solution:

Research remains structurally dominant.

Art provides differentiation.

Risk 3 — Over-design

Heavy animation could make the website feel like a design-school portfolio.

Solution:

Motion must remain subtle and purposeful.

Admissions readability comes first.

Risk 4 — “Multi-talented student” cliché

Simply showing research + art + dance + baking can appear unfocused.

Solution:

Use editorial writing and transitions to reveal recurring motivations across activities.

After the core website is complete, possible additions include:

richer EEG visualization

audio podcast excerpts

animated running data

art timeline

interactive personal archive

reading / thinking list

chronological “Things I Have Been Curious About” page

These should only be added if the primary application narrative remains clear.

36. Final Creative Direction

The final website should feel like:

a research notebook, an art magazine, and a personal diary meeting on the same page.

The structure should be rigorous enough for an admissions officer.

The visuals should be personal enough to feel unmistakably individual.

The academic projects establish credibility.

The social project establishes empathy.

Art establishes sensitivity.

Movement establishes discipline.

Everyday practices establish humanity.

Together, they should answer one larger question:

How does this person observe, understand, and respond to the world?
